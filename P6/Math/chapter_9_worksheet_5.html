The Cake Stall

At the school fair the cake stall sells 5, 10, 15, 20, … cakes in hours 1, 2, 3, 4, …
  • (a) Write the term-to-term rule for the number of cakes sold each hour.[1]
  • (b) Write the nth term for the number of cakes sold in hour n.[1]
  • (c) How many cakes are sold in hour 6?[1]
  • (d) How many cakes are sold in hour 10?[1]
  • (e) Each cake costs $4. How much do 7 cakes cost?[1]
  • (f) Amy pays $36 for cakes. How many cakes does she buy?[1]
  • (g) In which hour does the stall sell 45 cakes?[1]
  • (h) Write the money earned in hours 1 to 5, if every cake costs $4.[1]

The Games Booth

A player starts with 40 tokens. Each game costs 3 tokens.
  • (a) Write the number of tokens left after games 1, 2, 3 and 4.[1]
  • (b) Write the term-to-term rule.[1]
  • (c) How many tokens are left after 8 games?[1]
  • (d) After how many games does a player have 7 tokens left?[1]
  • (e) Can a player have exactly 20 tokens left? Explain.[1]
  • (f) How many games can a player play altogether with 40 tokens?[1]

The Raffle

Raffle tickets are sold in packs of 8. The number of tickets in n packs is 8n.
  • (a) How many raffle tickets are in 7 packs?[1]
  • (b) Mina has 96 tickets. How many packs is that?[1]
  • (c) Can Mina have exactly 100 tickets? Explain.[1]
  • (d) Write the 3rd, 4th and 5th terms of the sequence 8n.[1]
  • (e) The machine × 8 has the input 15. What is the output?[1]
  • (f) The machine × 8 has the output 200. What is the input?[1]

The Treasure Trail

The Red team visits stations 4, 11, 18, 25, … The Blue team visits stations 3, 11, 19, 27, …
  • (a) Write the term-to-term rule for the Red team.[1]
  • (b) Write the term-to-term rule for the Blue team.[1]
  • (c) What is the first station that both teams visit?[1]
  • (d) What is the next station that both teams visit?[1]
  • (e) Do both teams visit station 123? Show how you know.[2]
  • (f) Why are the stations that both teams visit always 56 apart?[1]
Hint: write out the next few stations for each team and look for numbers in both lists.

The Prize Wheel

The prize in dollars on day n of the fair is 6n + 9.
  • (a) What is the prize on day 1?[1]
  • (b) What is the prize on day 20?[1]
  • (c) On which day is the prize $99? Show your working.[2]
  • (d) Can the prize ever be exactly $200? Explain.[1]
  • (e) Pip says, “The prize on day 10 is double the prize on day 5.” Is Pip right? Show your working.[2]

The Score Machine

Points are turned into a score by this machine.
input → × 3 → − 5 → score
  • (a) What is the score when the input is 8?[1]
