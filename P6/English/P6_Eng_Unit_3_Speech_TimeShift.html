Mastering the Time Shift

Understanding Tense Backshifting and Deictic Amnesia in Reported Speech

1

The Backshift Principle

When we report what someone said, we are usually doing it after they said it. Because the "reporting" happens in the past, the grammar of the original sentence must be pushed one step backward in time. We call this The Backshift Principle.

Rule 1
Present Simple → Past Simple
"I eat apples," he said.
He said that he ate apples.
Rule 2
Present Continuous → Past Continuous
"I am running," she said.
She said that she was running.
Rule 3 (The tricky one!)
Past Simple → Past Perfect
"I won the race," he said.
He said that he had won the race.
Rule 4
Present Perfect → Past Perfect
"I have finished," she said.
She said that she had finished.
2

The Universal Truth Shield

Warning: Do not backshift everything!

If the original statement expresses a universal truth, a scientific fact, or a situation that remains demonstrably true at the exact moment of reporting, the tense remains anchored in the present.

Direct Speech "Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius," the scientist noted.
Correct Indirect Speech The scientist noted that water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.

(Water still boils at 100 degrees, so we don't say it 'boiled'.)

3

Curing "Deictic Amnesia"

"Deictic markers" are words that point to a specific time or place (like here, today, tomorrow). If you report a speech days later, saying "tomorrow" creates a temporal paradox! You must translate these markers to reflect the new time.

Direct Speech (The Utterance) Indirect Speech (The Record)
Now Then / At that moment
Today That day
Tonight That night
Yesterday The day before / The previous day
Tomorrow The next day / The following day
This / These That / Those
Here There
