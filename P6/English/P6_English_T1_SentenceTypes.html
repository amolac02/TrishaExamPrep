1 · Introduction

Why sentence types matter

Imagine writing this in your diary: “I woke up. I ran late. I forgot my bag. I reached school.” The message is clear, but it sounds flat. When you learn different sentence types, your writing becomes smoother, smarter, and more interesting.

Real-life use

You use sentence types when telling a story, answering comprehension questions, writing emails, or explaining your ideas in class. Good writers mix sentence types to control pace and meaning.

Exam advantage

In school assessments, sentence variety shows control. It helps you avoid repetition, improve grammar accuracy, and earn stronger writing marks.

2A · Core concept

Simple sentences

A simple sentence has one subject, one main verb, and one complete thought. It contains one independent clause, which means it can stand alone and make sense by itself.

Structure

Subject + Verb + Complete thought

[Birds] {fly.}
[She] {reads every night.}
[The old cat] {slept on the sofa.}
[My brother and I] {laughed loudly.}
Key idea

A simple sentence can be short or long. It is called simple because it has only one main clause, not because it has few words.

  • Fragment: “Ran to the bus stop.” → missing subject.
  • Incomplete thought: “The children in the park.” → missing verb.
  • Confusion: a long sentence with one clause is still simple.

More examples

The sun shone brightly.

Our class visited the museum.

The tiny puppy barked at the gate.

Medium examples

The excited crowd cheered for the team.

My neighbour grows roses in her garden.

The train arrived exactly on time.

Spot the subject

In “The train arrived exactly on time,” the subject is the train and the predicate is arrived exactly on time.

2B · Core concept

Compound sentences

A compound sentence joins two independent clauses. Each side can stand alone, but they are linked because the ideas are connected.

FANBOYS

Use coordinating conjunctions: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.

  • And adds information.
  • But shows contrast.
  • So shows result.
  • Or gives a choice.
  • Yet shows surprising contrast.
Structure

Independent clause + comma + FANBOYS + independent clause

I was hungry, so I made a sandwich.
Maya wanted to swim, but the pool was closed.
We can walk home, or we can wait for the bus.

Compare with simple

Simple: The rain stopped.

Compound: The rain stopped, and the children ran outside.

Common errors

  • Missing comma: “I was tired but I kept going.”
  • Run-on: “I was tired I kept going.”
  • Too many joined clauses in one sentence.

Extra examples

The bell rang, and the class went quiet.

He trained hard, yet he felt nervous.

Sara called me, so I answered at once.

2C · Core concept

Complex sentences

A complex sentence has one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. The dependent clause cannot stand alone, so it needs the main clause to complete the meaning.

Independent vs dependent

Independent clause: She went to bed.

Dependent clause: after she finished her homework

She went to bed after she finished her homework.
Subordinating conjunctions
  • Time: when, while, after, before, until
  • Reason: because, since, as
  • Contrast: although, though, even though
  • Condition: if, unless

Examples

Because it was raining, we stayed indoors.

Although she was tired, she finished the project.

When the bell rang, the students rushed out.

Clause placement

Because it was cold, I wore a jacket.

I wore a jacket because it was cold.

The meaning stays similar, but the emphasis changes.

Common errors

  • Using a dependent clause as a full sentence.
  • Forgetting the comma when the dependent clause comes first.
  • Confusing “because” sentences with compound sentences.
3 · Comparison

See the differences quickly

Use this table when revising. It helps you compare structure, clauses, examples, and clue words in one place.

Sentence type Structure Number of clauses Example Key identifiers
Simple One independent clause 1 The cat slept on the rug. One complete thought, no joining word needed
Compound Independent clause + comma + FANBOYS + independent clause 2 or more independent clauses The cat slept on the rug, but the dog stayed awake. FANBOYS, two complete ideas
Complex Independent clause + dependent clause 1 independent + 1 or more dependent Although the cat slept on the rug, the dog stayed awake. Because, although, when, if, while, unless

5 · Guided practice

10 practice questions

Try these questions on your own first. Then check the answers below.

1. The wind blew across the field. → Identify the sentence type.
2. I studied hard, so I passed the test. → Identify the sentence type.
3. Although it was late, she kept reading. → Identify the sentence type.
4. The dog barked, and the baby cried. → Identify the sentence type.
5. My cousins played football in the park. → Identify the sentence type.
6. Convert to a compound sentence: I was tired. I finished my homework.
7. Convert to a complex sentence: She was hungry. She waited for dinner.
8. Convert to a complex sentence: It rained heavily. We stayed inside.
9. Fill in the blank: I wanted to go outside, ______ the storm had started.
10. Fill in the blank: ______ you practise every day, you will improve.

Suggested answers

  • 1. Simple
  • 2. Compound
  • 3. Complex
  • 4. Compound
  • 5. Simple
  • 6. I was tired, but I finished my homework.
  • 7. Although she was hungry, she waited for dinner.
  • 8. Because it rained heavily, we stayed inside.
  • 9. but / because, depending on how you rewrite the sentence
  • 10. If
6 · Challenge

Higher-level thinking

These tasks test your correction skills, grammar knowledge, and ability to transform sentences carefully.

1. Correct the error

She was nervous but she smiled and she answered every question.

2. Rewrite

Change this into a complex sentence using although: The weather was terrible. The match continued.

3. Identify clauses

Unless the team practises regularly, they will not improve.

4. Add punctuation

Because the lights went out the children screamed.

5. Create your own

Write one simple, one compound, and one complex sentence about your favourite hobby.

7 · Real-life application

Paragraph writing task

Write a short paragraph about a memorable day. It could be Sports Day, a birthday, a school trip, or even a rainy afternoon at home.

  • Use at least 2 simple sentences.
  • Use at least 2 compound sentences.
  • Use at least 2 complex sentences.
  • Label each sentence S, C, or CX after writing.

Model paragraph

It was Sports Day at school. (S) The field looked bright, and the whole class felt excited. (C) Although I was nervous, I joined the running race. (CX) The whistle blew, and we ran as fast as we could. (C) I crossed the line first. (S) Because I had practised for weeks, I felt proud of myself. (CX)

8 & 9 · Revision and memory tricks

Quick revision notes

Use these points for fast review before a test or quiz.

Simple

  • One independent clause
  • One complete thought
  • Can be short or long
  • Example: The baby slept peacefully.

Compound

  • Two independent clauses
  • Joined by FANBOYS
  • Usually needs a comma before the conjunction
  • Example: I wanted to play, but I had homework.

Complex

  • One independent clause + one dependent clause
  • Uses words like because, although, when, if
  • Comma after the dependent clause if it comes first
  • Example: Because it was late, we went home.

Memory tricks

  • FANBOYS = For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So
  • Stand-alone test: can this clause make sense by itself?
  • Complex clue: words like because, although, when, while, if, unless

Sentence spotting in exams

  • If both halves can stand alone and a FANBOYS word joins them, it is probably compound.
  • If one part cannot stand alone, it is probably complex.
  • If there is only one main clause, it is simple.
